# Personal website of Bui Duc Thinh

Static site served by GitHub Pages from the root of `buiducthinh4869/buiducthinh4869.github.io` (branch `main`). Plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript. No build step, no framework. `.nojekyll` must stay.

## Files

- `index.html`, `research.html`, `writing.html`, `cv.html`: the four pages in the nav bar.
- `writing/<slug>/index.html`: one folder per blog post.
- `blog/*.md`: post sources in Markdown, one file per language.
- `assets/style.css`: all styles. `assets/site.js`: EN/VI switch. `assets/post.js`: renders a post and mounts Giscus comments.
- `assets/portrait.jpg`: 480 x 600 crop of `assets/photo.png` (the original, not published).
- `assets/favicon.svg`, `assets/mark.svg`: the isobar mark (favicon, divider between sections of a post).

## Bilingual rule

Every visible text exists twice: `<span data-l="en">…</span><span data-l="vi" lang="vi">…</span>` (or `p`, `h1`, etc.). CSS hides the inactive language through `html[data-lang]`. When adding or editing text, always write both versions. Paper titles stay in English in both languages. Each page's `<html>` carries `data-title-en` and `data-title-vi`.

## Design rules

- Colors: paper `#faf9f5`, ink `#23272b`, muted `#6b6f72`, rule `#e4e1d8`, accent `#1f5f7a` (links and isobar only), accent-soft `#c9dbe2`.
- Fonts: Newsreader (reading text, headings), Be Vietnam Pro (nav, labels, meta lines). Both support Vietnamese.
- One decorative element only: the isobar drawing on the home page and its small mark. Do not add other ornaments, cards, shadows, gradients or all-caps labels.
- Reading width is 40rem. Must work at 375px wide without horizontal scroll.
- Layout, breakpoint 1000px. Below it every page is one stacked 40rem column. From 1000px the container (`.wrap`, header and footer included) is 72rem:
  - Home: two columns in `.home-grid`. `.profile` (photo, name, field, bio, links) on the left, sticky (static when the viewport is under 800px high); `.home-main` (Research, Writing) on the right at 40rem.
  - Research, Writing, CV: any `main > .wrap` that has `h2` children becomes a grid. Each `h2` sits in a 14rem left rail and the element after it in the 40rem right column; the page title, lede, `figure`, `.cv-actions` and `.cv-frame` span the full width. Keep `h2` and its list as direct children of `.wrap`.
  - Blog post: `<main class="post-page">` keeps the single 40rem column.

## Adding a blog post

1. Put `blog/<name>_en.md` and/or `blog/<name>_vi.md` in place (first line `# Title`; it is dropped on render because the page shows the title).
2. Copy `writing/how-i-found-my-way-into-research/index.html` to `writing/<new-slug>/index.html`; change titles (EN and VI, including `data-title-*`), date, and `data-src-en` / `data-src-vi`. Leave out a `data-src-*` if that language does not exist; the page then shows the other language with a note.
3. Add a row to the list in `writing.html` (newest first, grouped by year) and replace the row on the home page so it shows the newest post. Tags EN/VI show which languages exist.

## Storm atlas (storms.html)

A page of its own, linked from the Research page by `<figure class="atlas-teaser">` (the picture `assets/storm_atlas_teaser.png` is a still of section 1, taken with motion paused; retake it when the data change). It is in the nav bar ("Storm atlas" / "Bản đồ bão", after Research) on every page. The page does not name the project or its co-authors as the source of the wind fields (Thinh, 2026-11-20). Audience: researchers worldwide, so it is global, with Vietnam as the default region. Plain language first: each section opens with a question (h2) and a one-paragraph "how to read" (`.at-how`); definitions, sources and limits sit in "About the data". On screens from 1000px each section head is two columns (`.at-head`: heading in 22rem, text in 40rem), aligned with the globe's list and map below.

1. Where do tropical storms form? (`atlas_overview.js`, `.at-overview`). Every track on an Equal Earth map centred on 165 E, coloured by genesis basin, basin names written on the map. Streaks run along the tracks (650, or 220 under 600px wide) while the map is on screen; never under reduced motion unless the reader presses Play; "Pause motion" stops them, and the still map then draws the tracks stronger. Hover or tap shows a basin card (storms, mean per season, busiest season, strongest storm or storms, count of category 3 or stronger, most storms near one country or territory in one season); click sets the region and scrolls to section 2. "Play 44 seasons": each season takes 3 s at Fast, 5 s at Medium (default) and 8 s at Slow (speed buttons, changeable mid-run); "From … to …" selects choose the seasons to play, storms start in their order within the season (July to June in the southern hemisphere) and leave their track behind; a counter shows the season and the storms so far. A table view of the basin numbers sits in a `details`. All numbers are counts of public IBTrACS storms computed in the browser from `index.json`; they are not exposure numbers.
2. Which storms came near a place? One search box (`atlas_core.js`, sticky at the top through sections 2 and 3) split in two: a basin select (`#at-basin`) and a country box (`#at-q`, countries and territories only), matched on word starts; choosing in one clears the other (the other language only from the start of the name); a few aliases (Hawaii, Okinawa, Hainan) offer the country that contains them. Storms are found by name in section 3 (`#sg-q`), across all regions. The region line shows the region and its storm count. Timeline (`atlas_timeline.js`): for a country, one dot per storm, x = date of closest approach, y = peak IBTrACS wind within 200 km (`nr`), on category bands; without `nr` it falls back to formation date and lifetime peak and says so. For a basin or the whole world, stacked bars of storms per season by category; a bar filters the list in section 3 to that season. Hover tooltips on dots and bars; click a dot to pick the storm.
3. One storm up close (`storm_globe.js`, `.storm-globe`): as described in "Storm globe" below, plus tooltips on tracks and list rows, isotach labels (17 m/s, 33, 43, 50, 58) when still, a soft halo under the night lights when still, and 0.05-degree light tiles once a 0.2-degree cell is wider than 2.5 px.

- Colours. Basins (validated with the dataviz skill's palette script: adjacent CVD and normal-vision checks pass in this order; names are on the map as a second cue): WP `#2a6fc0`, NA `#d9632e`, EP `#179a6c`, NI `#c98a00`, SI `#c95f8c`, SP `#5a4ac4`, SA `#6b6f72`. Categories, one warm hue light to dark: TS `#d3bb8f`, C1 `#c49a62`, C2 `#ad7a45`, C3 `#8f5a32`, C4 `#6b3c26`, C5 `#45231c`; timeline bands at 17, 33, 43, 50, 58, 70 m/s (the isotach levels), and a dot's colour follows the same edges. Selected storms on the globe: `#1f5f7a`, `#b08a5a`, `#7a4f6e`, `#4f6e3a`.
- State: region (`""`, `b:WP`, `c:<index>`), season filter and up to four selected storms live in `Atlas.st` and in the URL hash (`#r=vietnam&s=SID,SID`; `r=world`, `r=b-wp`), so every view can be shared. Default: Vietnam with Yagi 2024.
- Code: `atlas_core.js` (data, state, hash, search, tooltip, storm card), `atlas_overview.js`, `atlas_timeline.js`, `storm_globe.js`; D3 7.9.0 from cdnjs; each file under 40 KB unminified. CSS blocks "Storm atlas teaser", "Storm atlas (storms.html)" and "Storm globe and overview". Text in each file's `TXT`/`TX` table (en, vi) and in `data-l` spans; everything re-renders on `langchange`. Tooltip text is built with `textContent`.
- The old Vietnam explorer (`assets/storm_map.js`, `storm_map_static.png`, `assets/data/storms_vn.json`, `land_outline.json`, `lights_vn.*`, `wind/`) is on no page since 2026-11-20. Its data stay because the globe build checks itself against them.
- Rebuild, in this order: `python -E -P _materials/storm_map/build_storm_map.py` (IBTrACS selection, lights, land; about 1 minute), then `python -E -P _materials/storm_map/build_wind_fields.py` (wind fields, static picture; about 1 minute). Each asserts its checks and ends with DONE. `_materials/` is not published. The storm globe is built after them (it checks itself against `storms_vn.json` and `wind/`): `python -E -P _materials/storm_globe/build_globe.py` (about 5 minutes; the first run also reads the global night lights from the cold-store zip at 0.2 and 0.05 degree, about 15 minutes each, and caches them in `_materials/storm_globe/cache/`; it checks `nr` against `storms_vn.json` and the Damrey field against `wind/`).

- Wind fields come from the research project Cyclone-economic-growth, **with Toan Phan's approval of 2026-11-16, which covers wind-field images of individual storms only**. Method: the project's hourly points (`data/master/tracks_d1_hourly.csv`, `vmax_hybrid`), the main Rmax version c2r (`result/enso/d1/rmax_smoothing/series/rmax_c2r.parquet`, linear in time between synoptic fixes; seasons 2023-24 use its fallback formula, which is `rmax_km` of the hourly file), Holland (1980) with B = 1.5 and a 500 km cutoff (`pipeline.holland_wind`, imported), per-cell peak over the storm's life on the 0.1 degree grid, cropped to 100-118 E, 6-25 N. PNG value = m/s rounded down (so value >= 17 is exactly wind >= 17), 0 below 10 m/s, row 0 = north; `wind_bbox` = [west, south, east, north]. The build reproduces the project's Damrey 2017 figure numbers (43.95 m/s at the landfall cell, 798 / 204 / 0 Vietnam cells at 17 / 33 / 50 m/s) before using c2r (781 / 200 / 0).
- **Nothing else from that project may be published without co-author approval**: no project track points or `vmax_hybrid` winds (tracks, fixes and every wind number shown are public IBTrACS), no exposure numbers (wind x light, wind x land or area, light share), no ENSO results, no panels or estimates. The project folder and its cold store are read only; the build writes nothing there (bytecode writing is off when it imports the project's functions).
- No published file may carry a country border or country polygon: land is one dissolved, unlabelled shape, and country polygons and rasters exist only inside the build scripts. Since 2026-11-19 (Thinh's decision) country names may appear in one place only: the country list of the storm globe (`assets/data/globe/index.json`, field `countries`), used to filter storms and turn the globe. The Vietnam explorer stays without country names in its data files.

## Storm globe

`<figure class="storm-globe">` in section 3 of the atlas: an orthographic globe (drag to turn, wheel after the reader engages, + and − buttons) with night-time light, a list of storms (this region or anywhere; filtered by name, season range and category; sorted newest or strongest; 60 rows at a time with a "Show more" button), and up to four selected storms (track, 6-hourly dots, isotachs as lines, no fill). Play runs the selected storms together from each storm's first fix (60 ms of screen time per storm hour), the globe follows the first one, with growing isotachs (each cell reaches its published peak when the track passes closest, ramping up over the 9 hours before), eye rings, spiral bands (clockwise south of the equator) and dimming lights (an illustration, not measured outages); the clock is in UTC. Play runs even under reduced motion, because the reader starts it and can pause it.

- Wind fields worldwide: Thinh reported on 2026-11-19 that Toan Phan agrees to visualizing the project's wind fields on the site. Exposure numbers (StormActivity, LandExposure, wind x light, shares by basin), ENSO results and estimates still stay unpublished.
- Data contract, `assets/data/globe/` (built on Thinh's laptop by `_materials/storm_globe/build_globe.py`, not published):
  - `land.json`: Natural Earth 110m land, dissolved, GeoJSON MultiPolygon, coordinates rounded to 0.01 degree (built from the `world-atlas` npm package, land-110m).
  - `index.json`: `{"meta": {...}, "countries": [[name_en, name_vi, lon, lat, radius_deg], ...], "storms": [...]}`. `meta`: `source` (text), `seasons` [1981, 2024], `wind_res` (degrees per wind cell), `lights` {`bounds` [west, south, east, north], `cell_deg`, `width`, `height`, `product`, `year`, `scale` {`transform` "log1p", `max`, `ink_from`}}. `countries`: only countries with at least one storm; lon, lat = a point inside the country for turning the globe, on the part of the country (polygon) near the most storms (Réunion for France); radius_deg = half the larger side of that part's bounding box. `name_en` from Natural Earth `NAME_EN`, `name_vi` from `NAME_VI` (else `NAME_EN`), with a short override table in the build script (China, Micronesia; Myanmar, Brazil, Mexico, Vương quốc Anh, Triều Tiên and a few others in Vietnamese). The select labels the group "Countries and territories" / "Quốc gia và vùng lãnh thổ". Storm objects, sorted by SID: `id` (SID), `name` (IBTrACS NAME, title case; "Unnamed" for UNNAMED/NOT_NAMED), `season`, `basin` (IBTrACS BASIN of the first fix; MM folded into the basin of the next fix), `vmax_ms` (lifetime max of the wind defined below, integer m/s), `class` (TS, C1 to C5 from knots as in the Vietnam explorer), `t0` (ISO time of the first fix, `YYYY-MM-DDTHH:MMZ`), `near` (sorted indices into `countries` whose land lies within 200 km, haversine, of any fix), `nr` (same order as `near`: `[country index, peak wind m/s within 200 km of that country's land (integer, the largest IBTrACS wind among the fixes within 200 km; null if none of them has a wind), fractional year of the fix closest to that land (3 decimals)]`; for Vietnam it must equal `vmax_near_ms` and `t_near` of `storms_vn.json` for all 271 storms), and when a wind field exists `wind_bbox` [west, south, east, north] (cell edges). (`wind_max_ms` is no longer written: the page does not use it.)
  - Selection: IBTrACS v04r01 main tracks (`TRACK_TYPE` main), seasons 1981 to 2024, all basins, lifetime max wind at least 34 kt. Wind as in the Vietnam explorer: `USA_WIND` if the storm has any, else `WMO_WIND`, knots x 0.5144.
  - `tracks/<season>.json`: `{SID: [[hours since t0, lon, lat, wind m/s integer or null, (1 if the fix follows a gap of more than 6 hours)], ...]}`, every main-track fix in time order. Longitude is unwrapped along the track (continuous, may pass beyond 180 or -180), so lines across the date line stay straight.
  - `lite.json`: `{"q": 10, "tracks": [[x0, y0, dx1, dy1, ...], ...]}` in the order of `index.storms`, longitudes and latitudes in tenths of a degree (integers, unwrapped longitude), Douglas-Peucker simplified at 0.25 degree, deltas after the first point.
  - `wind/<SID>.png`: 8-bit grey, value = peak wind in m/s rounded down, 0 below 10 m/s, row 0 = north, cell size `wind_res` (0.1 degree since 2026-11-20, the same grid as the Vietnam explorer; the build keeps the folder under 45 MB, else 0.2), per-cell peak over the storm's life with the same method as the Vietnam explorer (project hourly points, Rmax c2r, Holland B = 1.5, 500 km cutoff), with no map window (cropped only to the cells of at least 10 m/s); `wind_bbox` in the same unwrapped longitude as the track. A field whose peak exceeds the storm's IBTrACS `vmax_ms` by more than 10 m/s is left out (34 storms, mostly extratropical stages near the date line where the project's wind sits far above IBTrACS; listed by the build). Storms the project does not cover get no `wind_bbox` either (31 of 3,921: no c2r value and a Xu Rmax at some fix, so the main Rmax version is not defined; storms without c2r whose Rmax is Willoughby throughout use `rmax_km`, the c2r fallback formula). The countries come from Natural Earth's default view; units of type Indeterminate or Lease (Antarctica, Spratly Is., Scarborough Reef and others) are left out, and the seven Paracel (Hoàng Sa) islets that Natural Earth puts inside China count for no country. `name` keeps IBTrACS's colon for renamed storms ("Adelaide:Alice").
  - `lights.png`: global night-time light, grey 8-bit, equirectangular, `cell_deg` 0.2, bounds [-180, -60, 180, 75], row 0 = north, value = round(255 x log1p(radiance) / max), 0 for sea and dark land; `ink_from` as in `lights_vn.json`. The script leaves out values below 24 when drawing. `meta.lights.tiles` = {`cell_deg` 0.05, `size_deg` 10, `list` ["<lon0>_<lat0>", ...]} lists the tiles `lights/<lon0>_<lat0>.png` (lon0 from -180 to 170, lat0 from -60 to 70, steps of 10): 200 x 200 grey 8-bit, covering [lon0, lat0, lon0 + 10, lat0 + 10], row 0 = north, the same value scale (same `max`) as `lights.png`; only tiles with at least one value of 24 or more are written and listed. A tile replaces the 0.2-degree cells inside it once loaded.
