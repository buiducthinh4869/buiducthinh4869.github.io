# Personal website of Bui Duc Thinh

Static site served by GitHub Pages from the root of `buiducthinh4869/buiducthinh4869.github.io` (branch `main`). Plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript. No build step, no framework. `.nojekyll` must stay.

## Files

- `index.html`, `research.html`, `writing.html`, `cv.html`: the four pages in the nav bar (with `storms.html` and `housing.html`, six links).
- `writing/<slug>/index.html`: one folder per blog post.

## Bilingual rule

Every visible text exists twice: `<span data-l="en">…</span><span data-l="vi" lang="vi">…</span>` (or `p`, `h1`, etc.). CSS hides the inactive language through `html[data-lang]`. When adding or editing text, always write both versions. Paper titles stay in English in both languages. Each page's `<html>` carries `data-title-en` and `data-title-vi`.

## Design rules

- Colors: paper `#faf9f5`, ink `#23272b`, muted `#6b6f72`, rule `#e4e1d8`, accent `#1f5f7a` (links and isobar only), accent-soft `#c9dbe2`.
- Fonts: Newsreader (reading text, headings), Be Vietnam Pro (nav, labels, meta lines). Both support Vietnamese.
- One decorative element only: the isobar drawing on the home page and its small mark. Do not add other ornaments, cards, shadows, gradients or all-caps labels.
- Reading width is 40rem. Must work at 375px wide without horizontal scroll.
- Layout, breakpoint 1000px. Below it every page is one stacked 40rem column. From 1000px the container (`.wrap`, header and footer included) is 72rem:
  - Home: two columns in `.home-grid`. `.profile` (photo, name, field, bio, links) on the left, sticky (static when the viewport is under 800px high); `.home-main` (Research, Writing) on the right at 40rem.
  - Research, Writing, CV: any `main > .wrap` that has `h2` children becomes a grid. Each `h2` sits in a 14rem left rail and the element after it in the 40rem right column; the page title, lede, `figure`, `.cv-actions` and `.cv-frame` span the full width. Keep `h2` and its list as direct children of `.wrap`.
  - Blog post: `<main class="post-page">` keeps the single 40rem column.

## Adding a blog post

1. Put `blog/<name>_en.md` and/or `blog/<name>_vi.md` in place (first line `# Title`; it is dropped on render because the page shows the title).
2. Copy `writing/how-i-found-my-way-into-research/index.html` to `writing/<new-slug>/index.html`; change titles (EN and VI, including `data-title-*`), date, and `data-src-en` / `data-src-vi`. Leave out a `data-src-*` if that language does not exist; the page then shows the other language with a note.
3. Add a row to the list in `writing.html` (newest first, grouped by year) and replace the row on the home page so it shows the newest post. Tags EN/VI show which languages exist.

## Storm atlas (storms.html)

A page of its own, linked from the Research page by `<figure class="at-overview atlas-teaser" data-ao-manual>`, which mounts the live section-1 renderer, not a copy (the pre-recorded video and still, `storm_atlas_teaser.mp4` and `.png`, were removed on 2026-11-24 because they drifted from the page): research.html loads D3 7.9.0, `atlas_core.js` and `atlas_overview.js`, then calls `Atlas.overview(figure, { play: false, href: "storms.html" })`. Same code, data (`index.json`, `land.json`, `lite.json`), constants and drawing as section 1 when idle: base tracks, streaks (650, or 220 under 600px wide) only while the map is on screen and never under reduced motion (the still map then draws the tracks stronger), basin names, legend with counts, hover card. Left out: Play and its controls, season range, counter, month bars, table. The one bar keeps the "Pause motion" link (dropped under reduced motion). A click on a basin or its legend entry opens `storms.html#r=b-<basin>`; a click elsewhere on the map and the "Open the storm atlas →" link open `storms.html`; the teaser never writes the URL hash. The map box reserves its 2:1 shape in CSS (block "Storm atlas teaser") so the page does not jump while the data load; without JS, or if the data fail, only the caption and link show. `Atlas.overview(figure, options)` mounts any figure of that markup: `play` (default true) and `href` (default none, which scrolls to section 2); storms.html mounts every `.at-overview` on load with the defaults, and a figure marked `data-ao-manual` is left to the caller. It is in the nav bar ("Storm atlas" / "Bản đồ bão", after Research) on every page. The page does not name the project or its co-authors as the source of the wind fields (Thinh, 2026-11-20). Audience: researchers worldwide, so it is global, with Vietnam as the default region. Plain language first: each section opens with a question (h2) and a one-paragraph "how to read" (`.at-how`); definitions, sources and limits sit in "About the data". On screens from 1000px each section head is two columns (`.at-head`: heading in 22rem, text in 40rem), aligned with the globe's list and map below.

1. Where do tropical storms form? (`atlas_overview.js`, `.at-overview`). Every track on an Equal Earth map centred on 165 E, coloured by genesis basin, basin names written on the map. Streaks run along the tracks (650, or 220 under 600px wide) while the map is on screen; never under reduced motion unless the reader presses Play; "Pause motion" stops them, and the still map then draws the tracks stronger. Hover or tap shows a basin card (storms, mean per season, busiest season, strongest storm or storms, count of category 3 or stronger, most storms near one country or territory in one season); click sets the region and scrolls to section 2. "Play 44 seasons": each month takes 1 to 20 s, set by a slider (5 s, one minute a year, by default; changeable mid-run); "From … to …" selects choose the seasons to play, storms start in their order within the season (July to June in the southern hemisphere) and cross their track within 0.8 of a season; Play runs by calendar year (January to December in both hemispheres; a storm counts in the year it formed): the map shows only the current year (cleared at each new year); the counter shows year, month and the year's storm count; each basin name on the map carries two counts underneath, storms formed in that month (large, "2 storms in Jul 1985") and that year (small, "Total: 25 storms in 1985"); the counter shows the same for all basins (the legend then shows names only); twelve small month bars (bottom left of the map) grow as storms form, the current month dark. Two clocks: "Seconds per month" (1 to 20 s, 5 s default) moves the month; "Seconds per storm loop" (1 to 10 s, 3 s default) is how long one storm takes to run its whole track; the storms formed in the shown month loop along their tracks until the month ends (also while paused), and earlier months of the year stay as faint tracks. Basin names and the month count on the map are in the basin colour (darkened for text). Under 600 px wide the map keeps only the basin names during Play; the counts move to the legend, one line per basin ("Western Pacific · 4 in Sep 1981 · 29 in 1981"), and the month bars sit under the map. Play works like a video: the state at play time t is computed from t alone (`stateAt`), so Pause/Resume, ◀ 1 month / 1 month ▶, a scrubber over every month of the range, and Stop all work, also backwards. When not playing, the counter shows the range total and the legend the period total per basin. A table view of the basin numbers sits in a `details`. All numbers are counts of public IBTrACS storms computed in the browser from `index.json`; they are not exposure numbers. Night lights (added 2026-11-24; the same layer on Research, whose map is this code): the yearly image of `assets/ntl/` sits under the tracks, on top of the land fill, drawn by `atlas_overview.js` through its own `Atlas.ntlLayer` controller (separate from the globe's), at constant opacity `NTL_ALPHA` (0.55) and the globe's colour ramp (`Atlas.ntl.colors`; below grey 24 nothing). Not playing it is 2025; during Play (also paused, stepped or scrubbed) it is `yearFor` the calendar year the map shows (1992 for earlier years, with the note); Stop, the end of the range and a From/To change bring 2025 back; crossfade 300 ms, none under reduced motion; a failed image keeps the layer on screen and shows no error. Projection by a lookup table (pixel to image cell, built once per canvas size in CSS pixels; Equal Earth rows have one latitude, so two `proj.invert` calls per row give the whole row; about 10 ms at 1280 px), the image max-pooled 2 x 2 so a lit cell is not lost where a pixel is wider than a cell. The key line `p.ao-ntl` ("Night lights: 2025", EN and VI) is created by the script right after `.ao-map`. `ntl_layer.js` loads before `atlas_overview.js` on both pages.
2. Which storms came near a place? One search box (`atlas_core.js`, sticky at the top through sections 2 and 3) split in two: a basin select (`#at-basin`) and a country box (`#at-q`, countries and territories only), matched on word starts; choosing in one clears the other (the other language only from the start of the name); a few aliases (Hawaii, Okinawa, Hainan) offer the country that contains them. Storms are found by name in section 3 (`#sg-q`), across all regions. The region line shows the region and its storm count. Timeline (`atlas_timeline.js`): for a country, one dot per storm, x = date of closest approach, y = peak IBTrACS wind within 200 km (`nr`), on category bands; without `nr` it falls back to formation date and lifetime peak and says so. For a basin or the whole world, stacked bars of storms per season by category; a bar filters the list in section 3 to that season. Hover tooltips on dots and bars; click a dot to pick the storm.

## Storm globe

`<figure class="storm-globe">` in section 3 of the atlas: an orthographic globe (drag to turn, wheel after the reader engages, + and − buttons) with night-time light, a list of storms (this region or anywhere; filtered by name, season range and category; sorted newest or strongest; 60 rows at a time with a "Show more" button), and up to four selected storms (track, 6-hourly dots, isotachs as lines, no fill). Play runs the selected storms together from each storm's first fix (a "Seconds per storm day" slider, 0.5 to 6 s, 3 s default, changeable mid-run), the globe follows the first one; Play/Pause, Stop (back to the start and to the 2025 lights), ◀ 6 h / 6 h ▶ (each step lands on the next or previous 6-hourly synoptic time of the first storm, a real IBTrACS record) and a scrubber over the storm hours all work, also before the first Play and backwards (`apply` draws the state at any play time); with growing isotachs (each cell reaches its published peak when the track passes closest, ramping up over the 9 hours before), eye rings, spiral bands (clockwise south of the equator) and dimming lights (an illustration, not measured outages); the clock line (time and IBTrACS wind) shows the latest 6-hourly synoptic fix and changes only every 6 storm hours, so it can be read; it is in UTC. Play runs even under reduced motion, because the reader starts it and can pause it.

- Night-light years (added 2026-11-24). Not playing (first view, after Stop or reset, when no storm is selected), the globe shows `lights.png` (2025) with the 0.05 degree tiles, exactly as before. While a play-time state is on screen (Play, Pause, a step with ◀ 6 h / 6 h ▶ or the scrubber, also before the first Play), the season of the first selected storm (the one the globe follows) picks the image: `Atlas.ntl.yearFor(season)` (`assets/ntl_layer.js`, loaded before `storm_globe.js`. `Atlas.ntl` holds only what is shared and has no consumer state: `yearFor`, the one `index.json` promise (`load`, `retry`), the PNG decoder (`decode`), the raw-pixel cache (`pixels(year)`, 4 years, requests de-duplicated), the key text (`keyText(year, pre, lang)`, EN and VI) and the crossfade timing (`FADE` 300 ms, `fadeU`). `Atlas.ntlLayer(env)` returns an independent controller (`sync`, `reset`, `paint`, `cur`, `year`, `pre`, `from`, `def`) with the lazy loading, prefetch, layer cache, swap and crossfade; two consumers on one page do not share state. `storm_globe.js` passes it `lead`, `build`, `draw`, `redraw`, `reduce`, `changed` and `defaultLayer` (the 2025 layer with the tiles), listed in the module's header comment; the layer object is opaque to the module) is the largest year in `assets/ntl/index.json` `years` that is not after the season, the earliest year if the season is earlier, the latest if it is later. A year missing from the list is skipped by this rule, so the key always names the real image year. When the year changes, `ntl_<year>.png` loads lazily (the index only on first use; the next 1 to 2 years in the list are prefetched; at most 4 layers besides the 2025 one are kept) and crossfades in over 300 ms (no crossfade under reduced motion); a failed index or image leaves the current layer and raises no error (tried again at the next Play). The Stop button (next to Play, shown once a play-time state is on screen; "Stop" / "Dừng", class `se-link` as in section 1) calls `stopPlay(true)`: the storms go back to their start and the globe to 2025 with the tiles. Any change of the selection, also deselecting all storms, does the same. When play ends by itself the last state, and its year, stay on screen. The 0.05 degree tiles exist for 2025 only, so they are neither loaded nor drawn while another year is shown. The key on the globe (bottom right) reads "Night lights: 2005" / "Ánh sáng ban đêm: 2005", and for seasons before the earliest image "Night lights: 1992 (no earlier satellite image)" / "Ánh sáng ban đêm: 1992 (không có ảnh vệ tinh sớm hơn)"; it follows `langchange`. Twinkle takes its points from the image being shown: they are collected while the cells are drawn (`dots`), so they always belong to the layer on screen (`dots(..., keep)`: only that layer, or the incoming one during a fade, feeds twinkle); the halo and the dimming read only the drawn canvas and the storm. The Cells (0.1°) view is in `assets/globe_cells.js` (`Atlas.cells`: `draw`, `tip`, `key`; its text in its own table; `storm_globe.js` hands it `Atlas.cells.env = {G, st, fields, vis}`). If either module is missing, the globe is hidden and the atlas failure note shows. Sizes on disk (CRLF), to keep each file under 40 KB: `storm_globe.js` 39,338 B (it was 40,397 B in LF before this branch), `ntl_layer.js` 8,000 B, `globe_cells.js` 3,809 B.
- Data contract of `assets/ntl/` (built apart from `build_globe.py`, still growing: the code reads the years from `index.json` and never hard-codes them): `index.json` `{years: [...], earliest, latest, default: 2025, file: "ntl_{year}.png", bounds, cell_deg: 0.2, width: 1800, height: 675, scale (the same log1p max as `lights.png`), product, doi, source}`; `ntl_<year>.png`: 1800 x 675 grey 8-bit, same grid, bounds and value scale as `lights.png`, which is the 2025 layer (the code checks the image size). Product: NPP-VIIRS-like Version 2 (Chen et al., Harvard Dataverse, doi:10.7910/DVN/YGIVCD), one image per year, 1992 to 2025 when complete. The text in "About the data" of `storms.html` says this and the pairing rule.
- Wind fields worldwide: Thinh reported on 2026-11-19 that Toan Phan agrees to visualizing the project's wind fields on the site. Exposure numbers (StormActivity, LandExposure, wind x light, shares by basin), ENSO results and estimates still stay unpublished.
- Data contract, `assets/data/globe/` (built on Thinh's laptop by `_materials/storm_globe/build_globe.py`, not published):
  - `land.json`: Natural Earth 110m land, dissolved, GeoJSON MultiPolygon, coordinates rounded to 0.01 degree (built from the `world-atlas` npm package, land-110m).
  - `index.json`: `{"meta": {...}, "countries": [[name_en, name_vi, lon, lat, radius_deg], ...], "storms": [...]}`. `meta`: `source` (text), `seasons` [1981, 2024], `wind_res` (degrees per wind cell), `lights` {`bounds` [west, south, east, north], `cell_deg`, `width`, `height`, `product`, `year`, `scale` {`transform` "log1p", `max`, `ink_from`}}. `countries`: only countries with at least one storm; lon, lat = a point inside the country for turning the globe, on the part of the country (polygon) near the most storms (Réunion for France); radius_deg = half the larger side of that part's bounding box. `name_en` from Natural Earth `NAME_EN`, `name_vi` from `NAME_VI` (else `NAME_EN`), with a short override table in the build script (China, Micronesia; Myanmar, Brazil, Mexico, Vương quốc Anh, Triều Tiên and a few others in Vietnamese). The select labels the group "Countries and territories" / "Quốc gia và vùng lãnh thổ". Storm objects, sorted by SID: `id` (SID), `name` (IBTrACS NAME, title case; "Unnamed" for UNNAMED/NOT_NAMED), `season`, `basin` (IBTrACS BASIN of the first fix; MM folded into the basin of the next fix), `vmax_ms` (lifetime max of the wind defined below, integer m/s), `class` (TS, C1 to C5 from knots as in the Vietnam explorer), `t0` (ISO time of the first fix, `YYYY-MM-DDTHH:MMZ`), `near` (sorted indices into `countries` whose land lies within 200 km, haversine, of any fix), `nr` (same order as `near`: `[country index, peak wind m/s within 200 km of that country's land (integer, the largest IBTrACS wind among the fixes within 200 km; null if none of them has a wind), fractional year of the fix closest to that land (3 decimals)]`; for Vietnam it must equal `vmax_near_ms` and `t_near` of `storms_vn.json` for all 271 storms), and when a wind field exists `wind_bbox` [west, south, east, north] (cell edges). (`wind_max_ms` is no longer written: the page does not use it.)
  - Selection: IBTrACS v04r01 main tracks (`TRACK_TYPE` main), seasons 1981 to 2024, all basins, lifetime max wind at least 34 kt. Wind as in the Vietnam explorer: `USA_WIND` if the storm has any, else `WMO_WIND`, knots x 0.5144.
  - `tracks/<season>.json`: `{SID: [[hours since t0, lon, lat, wind m/s integer or null, (1 if the fix follows a gap of more than 6 hours)], ...]}`, every main-track fix in time order. Longitude is unwrapped along the track (continuous, may pass beyond 180 or -180), so lines across the date line stay straight.
  - `lite.json`: `{"q": 10, "tracks": [[x0, y0, dx1, dy1, ...], ...]}` in the order of `index.storms`, longitudes and latitudes in tenths of a degree (integers, unwrapped longitude), Douglas-Peucker simplified at 0.25 degree, deltas after the first point.
  - `wind/<SID>.png`: 8-bit grey, value = peak wind in m/s rounded down, 0 below 10 m/s, row 0 = north, cell size `wind_res` (0.1 degree since 2026-11-20, the same grid as the Vietnam explorer; the build keeps the folder under 45 MB, else 0.2), per-cell peak over the storm's life with the same method as the Vietnam explorer (project hourly points, Rmax c2r, Holland B = 1.5, 500 km cutoff), with no map window (cropped only to the cells of at least 10 m/s); `wind_bbox` in the same unwrapped longitude as the track. A field whose peak exceeds the storm's IBTrACS `vmax_ms` by more than 10 m/s is left out (34 storms, mostly extratropical stages near the date line where the project's wind sits far above IBTrACS; listed by the build). Storms the project does not cover get no `wind_bbox` either (31 of 3,921: no c2r value and a Xu Rmax at some fix, so the main Rmax version is not defined; storms without c2r whose Rmax is Willoughby throughout use `rmax_km`, the c2r fallback formula). The countries come from Natural Earth's default view; units of type Indeterminate or Lease (Antarctica, Spratly Is., Scarborough Reef and others) are left out, and the seven Paracel (Hoàng Sa) islets that Natural Earth puts inside China count for no country. `name` keeps IBTrACS's colon for renamed storms ("Adelaide:Alice").
  - `lights.png`: global night-time light (the 2025 layer; during Play the images of `assets/ntl/` take its place, see "Night-light years"), grey 8-bit, equirectangular, `cell_deg` 0.2, bounds [-180, -60, 180, 75], row 0 = north, value = round(255 x log1p(radiance) / max), 0 for sea and dark land; `ink_from` as in `lights_vn.json`. The script leaves out values below 24 when drawing. `meta.lights.tiles` = {`cell_deg` 0.05, `size_deg` 10, `list` ["<lon0>_<lat0>", ...]} lists the tiles `lights/<lon0>_<lat0>.png` (lon0 from -180 to 170, lat0 from -60 to 70, steps of 10): 200 x 200 grey 8-bit, covering [lon0, lat0, lon0 + 10, lat0 + 10], row 0 = north, the same value scale (same `max`) as `lights.png`; only tiles with at least one value of 24 or more are written and listed. A tile replaces the 0.2-degree cells inside it once loaded.
